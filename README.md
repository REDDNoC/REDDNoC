# Daniel Brenes | REDDNoC

Founder & CEO building **CRYPTØBEAM**, **MIOS (Market Intelligent OS)**, and **CRYPTØBEAM-PCST**.

## Current work

### CRYPTØBEAM
Compliance-first digital asset marketplace infrastructure.

Current Phase 1 architecture is **provider-led**: third-party providers perform regulated or asset-handling functions such as custody, identity/KYC, execution, and settlement. CRYPTØBEAM focuses on orchestration, integrations, workflow controls, auditability, reconciliation, and the customer/operator experience.

Core engineering direction:
- Java + Spring Boot microservices
- OpenHFT / Chronicle event backbone
- append-only audit trails and deterministic reconciliation
- provider integrations behind explicit service boundaries
- security, observability, policy enforcement, and operational controls

### CRYPTØBEAM-PCST
**Post-Quantum Proof-Carrying State Transport** — a new protocol research/codebase focused on self-authenticating state transport, fail-closed verification, capability containment, delegation safety, and post-quantum cryptographic agility.

PCST is **not** a rename or continuation of the older DAMA/Damaverse utility work. DAMA/Damaverse repositories are separate historical projects/evidence unless explicitly incorporated through reviewed, documented migration.

Current research direction includes:
- proof-carrying state envelopes
- explicit protocol invariants and threat models
- ML-KEM / ML-DSA-aligned post-quantum suites
- non-normative rail mappings, including XRPL research
- transport abstractions spanning terrestrial and delay/disruption-tolerant links

### MIOS
**Market Intelligent OS** — a separate private research and intelligence platform focused on market analysis, decision support, and AI-assisted workflows.

## Engineering principles

- Evidence before claims
- Runtime validation over compile-only confidence
- Fail closed at trust and policy boundaries
- No hardcoded secrets; environment/vault-backed configuration
- Strong reconciliation, provenance, observability, and auditability
- Historical repositories are evidence sources, not automatically canonical implementations
